## Problem Addressed
Gomory's group/corner-polyhedron view (the integer hull seen modulo the lattice generated by the basis) is the theoretically cleanest explanation of why GMI/MIR cuts work, but it had been largely supplanted by disjunctive proofs; practitioners lacked a modern statement of what group cuts buy.

## Core Contribution
- **Methodology:** Survey of the group (corner polyhedron) formulation: project the integer set onto the lattice induced by a basis, describe its hull with group inequalities, and relate them back to GMI/split/lift-and-project cuts.
- **Assumptions:** Bounded ILP, a given basis/projection; theory-level treatment.
- **Benchmarks/datasets:** Illustrative examples; survey.
- **Metrics:** Group rank, strength relative to disjunctive cuts.
- **Key results:** Explains the mechanism behind our implemented cuts and unifies the fractional-cut (Module 8) and disjunctive (split) views.

## Engineering-Relevant Knowledge
**Algorithms:** Group (corner-polyhedron) cuts; relationship of GMI to the shortest-lattice-vector / knapsack-group description.
**Techniques:** Basis-dependent projection; explains why cut strength depends on the LP basis we happen to pick (basis choice = cut quality).
**Implementation details:** Nothing to ship directly; it justifies investing in basis-quality and cut-selection work in `src/milp/gomory.cpp` rather than inventing new families.
**Equations/rules:** Corner polyhedron P* = conv{ y ∈ Z^n : y ≡ q mod Q } style description; inequalities come from the knapsack over the group.
**Limitations/failure cases:** Group hull computation is NP-hard in general; theory can oversell strength that floating-point separation cannot realize.
